Alright, let's get started with today's lecture on machine learning.
Last week we talked about linear regression, so today we will move on to gradient descent.
The idea is simple: we want to find the parameters that minimize the loss function.
Imagine you are standing on a hill in thick fog, and you want to walk down to the lowest point.
At each step, you look at the slope under your feet and take a small step downhill.
The size of that step is called the learning rate.
If the learning rate is too large, you will overshoot the minimum and the training will diverge.
If it is too small, training will take forever to converge.
In practice, we usually start with a learning rate of zero point zero zero one and adjust from there.
Now, who can tell me what overfitting means?
Overfitting happens when the model memorizes the training data instead of learning general patterns.
A common sign is that the training loss keeps going down while the validation loss starts going up.
To reduce overfitting, we can use regularization, dropout, or simply collect more data.
We also split our dataset into three parts: training, validation, and test.
A typical split is seventy percent for training, fifteen percent for validation, and fifteen percent for test.
Remember, you should never tune your hyperparameters on the test set.
